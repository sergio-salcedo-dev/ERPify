- (decision) D1/D2 upload transport (user): evidence is uploaded in two steps. Step 1 declares size (and optionally sha256), carries the actor-scoped idempotency key and creates the upload-intent record; a zero or over-MAX_ORIGINAL_BYTES declared size is refused there, before any keystore row, so it leaves no tombstone. Step 2 is a raw-body PUT sealed as it is read; Content-Length is mandatory (411 otherwise) and must equal the declared size, and the bytes counted at end of stream must equal it, else the upload falls into the aborted-upload class. No unknown-length stream exists, so a body Caddy cuts off can never read as a complete shorter original, and no multipart UploadedFile spool exists for evidence.
- (decision) Consequences of D1/D2 (assistant derivation): the UNIQUE on the actor-scoped idempotency key is taken at step 1, so two concurrent first uploads no longer both stream (the cost SPEC accepted is removed); the Caddy bound is scoped to the upload route (the 1MB request_body block on the front controller must not also cap it) and equals the cap in bytes since the body is the original, with no framing allowance; per-actor rate limit on step 1 through the existing limiter pattern, no in-flight quota (YAGNI); aborted-upload tombstones are never pruned and a growth metric is added; a spike before doc-3 measures FrankenPHP and PHP behaviour with a raw body (php://input, post_max_size, request time limit), which is not verified.
- (decision) D3 scan retry (user, narrows the earlier 'unavailable burns no attempt'): unavailable means only 'before any content was sent'. A health probe runs before the claim, and a down scanner means nothing is claimed (no epoch, no attempt, no custody row). One counter scan_attempts on the snapshot counts claims that began streaming and ended with no verdict (mid-stream reset, scanner timeout, scanner crash, worker death, lease expiry, deadline). An absolute per-claim deadline that the heartbeat compare-and-set cannot extend (it requires now < claimed_at + deadline) bounds a poison document. A sweep on next_scan_at in PostgreSQL is authoritative and drives retries; the Messenger message only wakes it. The single counter is the ceiling on claims since the last operator decision.
