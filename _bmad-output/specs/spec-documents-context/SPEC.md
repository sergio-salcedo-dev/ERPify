> **Canonical contract.** This SPEC and the files in `companions:` are the complete, preservation-validated contract for what to build, test, and validate. Source documents listed in frontmatter are for traceability — consult them only if you need narrative rationale or prose color this contract intentionally omits.

# Documents bounded context — evidence-class binaries

## Why

A mandate and a vision. The ERP targets construction, which produces technical documentation whose **original byte is the proof**: drawings, reports, contracts, certificates, RAW, and the site photograph attached to a progress certificate. The images module re-encodes and strips on purpose, so it destroys exactly what makes these evidence. Nothing in the tree accepts evidence yet: the images pipeline rejects non-image formats, but a site JPEG passes `UploadImage` and is stripped (the ADR D3 misfiling hazard). Guidelines are fixed now so that the first `Documents` story inherits decided boundaries and named open questions instead of rediscovering them. Boundary authority: the accepted ADR in `companions:`.

## Capabilities

- **CAP-1**
  - **intent:** A caller can file an evidence-class binary, which is stored untransformed with its digest, actor, date, legal basis and retention policy recorded.
  - **success:** The bytes read back and decrypted equal the uploaded bytes, and the recorded digest equals the digest of the uploaded bytes. A truncated or aborted upload leaves no document and no object in the final namespace, and its key is destroyed within the reaper bound. The acceptance reads through the storage port and the decryptor in a test, since ingest exposes no read route.
- **CAP-2**
  - **intent:** A document is consumable only once it is scanned and ready; scanning classifies and never cleans.
  - **success:** A read in `Received`, `Scanning`, `Quarantined` or `ScanFailed` is refused with a defined RFC 9457 problem type; only a read in `Ready` returns bytes.
- **CAP-3**
  - **intent:** Originals are sealed at rest under a key scoped to the document, with peak memory independent of the size of the original.
  - **success:** The peak memory of sealing an original of 1 MiB and one of `MAX_ORIGINAL_BYTES` (measured, not asserted) differs by less than a bound doc-1 fixes by measurement, and both decrypt back byte-identical.
- **CAP-4**
  - **intent:** Erasure ends the conservation contract by destroying the document's key, from any non-terminal state, leaving digest and custody record intact.
  - **success:** After erasure the content cannot be decrypted, sealing under the same scope is refused, and digest and custody remain readable.
- **CAP-5**
  - **intent:** Derivatives of a document (previews) are materialised through the images module with their origin recorded, and die with it.
  - **success:** After erasure, the variant URL answers 404 or 410, no `Image` row with that origin remains, and no storage key for it remains; a derivative that arrives after erasure is deleted on arrival.
- **CAP-6**
  - **intent:** Each state change of a document is recorded, and the document's metadata and custody trail can be listed in order.
  - **success:** The ordered sequence of states of one document can be listed, including after erasure.
- **CAP-7**
  - **intent:** A new version is a new immutable original linked to its predecessor, never a modification.
  - **success:** Both versions are retrievable with distinct digests until erased; erasing one leaves the other's link to it as a readable tombstone.
- **CAP-8**
  - **intent:** Scanned PDFs yield a searchable text derivative.
  - **success:** A phrase present only in a scanned page is found by search; the text derivative dies with its document.
- **CAP-9**
  - **intent:** An authorised user can read a `Ready` document, and the read is audited.
  - **success:** The route declares its voter, refuses non-`Ready` states (CAP-2), writes an audit row, and serves the bytes with `nosniff` and a locked-down `Content-Security-Policy`.

## Constraints

- **Decided (the user, 2026-10-30): state-oriented persistence plus a custody log.** `Document` is a snapshot row; every transition also appends a `document_custody` row in the same transaction, except the scan lease returns named under Custody guarantees. Event sourcing was rejected on measurement: the event store has no per-aggregate stream loading (`DbalEventStore::stream()` is a global, sequence-ordered projection feed), and its `aggregate_version` is informative (`DbalEventStore::append()` computes `MAX+1`, under a `UNIQUE (tenant_id, aggregate_id, aggregate_version)` from `api/migrations/2026/Version20260616201857.php` that never fires because `tenant_id` is always `NULL` and PostgreSQL treats `NULL`s as distinct), so it would first need aggregate loading, optimistic concurrency and a serialised append. `document_custody` is not `audit_log`: reads stay in `audit_log`, which has a retention prune; custody never expires.
- **Custody guarantees.** The aggregate is loaded under the lock (`PESSIMISTIC_WRITE`, or re-read after `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`), so the compare-and-set never runs against a stale snapshot; `seq` is computed inside that same transaction. `UNIQUE (document_id, seq)` is the second defence, not a substitute, and a violation maps to a retry, never a 500. The first row (`seq` 1) has no row to lock: the document insert and its first custody row share one transaction and only the `UNIQUE` guards it. Lock order is document, then keystore, within one document; ingest and a new version lock only the keystore row of the new, not-yet-visible scope before their own insert and never the keystore row of an existing document without that document first. A flow that locks several documents takes them in ascending id, custody rows included; a series erasure takes one document per transaction. A repeated erasure appends nothing. `Erased` is a snapshot state, never a row delete, and the custody foreign key has no cascade. Custody rows are never deleted and are updated by no statement except the single sanctioned actor-anonymisation one decided below. **Enforcement is a source gate over `api/src` in the style of `SanctionedLogMutationGateTest`, and nothing more:** no database role separation, `GRANT`, `REVOKE` or trigger exists in the tree, the gate sees neither a migration nor runtime-built SQL, and adding a restricted role or a `BEFORE UPDATE/DELETE` trigger is a separate decision. Custody stays readable after the key is destroyed and is not claimed cryptographically immutable. Custody records the first scan claim since the last operator decision, every verdict (including `ScanFailed` reached by the attempt cap or by a deterministic failure) and every operator decision; a lease return (`Scanning` back to `Received`) and a re-claim are counted on the snapshot and write no custody row, an explicit exemption from "every transition appends" that the ordered listing of CAP-6 accepts.
- **Decided: a version is its own `Document` aggregate.** It carries its own bytes, digest, key scope, retention, scan lifecycle and custody. It links to its predecessor by an immutable id and to its series by a scalar series id (the first version's series id is its own id); both columns exist from the first migration. No series aggregate exists and a read projection lists versions. A consumer references the exact version it relied on. **The accepted chain is linear; failed attempts are siblings:** a partial unique index on `predecessor_id` that excludes children in `Quarantined`, `ScanFailed` or `Erased` refuses a second live child, and the use case authorises the caller on the predecessor (a voter) before sealing and answers "absent" and "forbidden" with the same problem type, so the conflict response leaks neither existence nor state; the short transaction that inserts a new version also takes `FOR UPDATE` on the predecessor row (after the new scope's keystore row, per the lock order above) and rechecks that it is `Ready`, because the predecessor's state when the upload began proves nothing; the lock is taken after the new bytes are sealed, never across the stream, so an upload cannot block the predecessor's erasure. The loser of the index race has already sealed bytes and a key: it falls into the aborted-upload class and maps to a conflict. A tombstone keeps id, digest, `series_id`, `predecessor_id`, state and timestamps, which name no person; everything else is gone or sealed. A child that ended `Quarantined`, `ScanFailed` or `Erased` frees the slot, so a corrected version is filed as a sibling, the series projection orders by creation and not by chain position, and reopening a `ScanFailed` document whose slot a replacement already holds violates the index and maps to a conflict. An `Erased` middle node blocks a successor (it needs a `Ready` predecessor) and the projection renders the gap.
- **Decided: the first evidence adapter is the local filesystem behind the `Documents` port**, and the port declares no WORM guarantee an adapter cannot enforce. S3 with object-lock waits for a first consumer and a defined retention vocabulary; the port keeps the move reversible (ADR D6). **Before first real use** (owned by doc-3, verified before doc-5 serves real documents) the stored bytes need an encrypted offsite backup, digest verification and a proven restore of originals and metadata: a database dump is not a backup of the evidence. **Ordering on the local filesystem.** Before any byte is sealed, a durable upload-intent record (created by the declare step of the transport decision; mechanism chosen in doc-3) holds the document's scope id, the actor, a lease expiry and a heartbeat. The scope id is random, is the document's own from that moment, is stored on the row under a `UNIQUE` (two documents sharing a scope would let one's erasure shred the other's bytes), and the staging key embeds it, so promotion needs no re-seal; the stream seal's associated data binds the scope, never a storage path. The seal mints the key under that scope and commits on its own: no database transaction spans the stream, and a long upload renews its lease by heartbeat or the lease outlasts the bound on upload time. Staging sits on the same mount as the final namespace (`rename(2)` is atomic only within one filesystem) with the file and its directory synced; the adapter compares `st_dev` of the two roots at boot and refuses to start otherwise, because on two volumes every promotion fails with `EXDEV` after the commit. A short transaction then locks the intent row, then the keystore row of the scope (through a `Keystore` method added in doc-1 that locks the row and reports live, tombstoned or absent, since `wrappedDekFor()` filters tombstones out and cannot tell them from absence), asserts that the intent lease is live and the scope is not tombstoned, inserts the `Received` row and its first custody row, and commits; the object is renamed to final and the final directory synced after that, and the scan message is dispatched only after the rename succeeds. **The intent row is the one lock the commit and the reaper share.** The reaper locks the intent row, then the keystore row, rechecks under both locks that the lease has expired and that no `Document` row holds the scope, and only then tombstones and deletes the intent in the same transaction; the commit locks them in the same order. Lease expiry is judged by the database clock (`NOW()`), never the application's. A `NOT EXISTS` inside the reaper's `UPDATE` would not do, because under `READ COMMITTED` its snapshot predates a row that commits while the `UPDATE` waits on the lock. A writer whose heartbeat finds its intent gone or expired aborts and deletes its own staged file, and an age sweep over the staging namespace deletes any staged object older than the upload bound plus a margin that has neither a live intent nor a row. **A committed row owns its staged object:** the reaper acts on intents, never deletes a staged object whose scope has a row and never destroys that scope; a sweep rolls the promotion forward for a `Received` row older than a bound whose object is only staged (the rename is idempotent and accepts the final object already present) and then dispatches the scan. The roll-forward sweep acts on `Received` rows only and skips an `Erased` one: the object of an `Erased` row belongs to the delete step decided below, and a roll-forward rename rechecks under the document row lock that the row is still `Received`. A final object with no row cannot arise in the normal flow (the row commits first); it appears after a restore to an older dump or by hand, so the sweeps **alarm on it and delete nothing in the final namespace for a scope that has no row**, and the restore step disables the sweeps and the reaper along with the writers. The one deletion in the final namespace is the `Erased` delete step, keyed on an `Erased` row. The sweeps also alarm on rows with no object anywhere, except `Erased` rows. The stream seal authenticates truncation (the last chunk carries a final-chunk marker in its associated data), so a short file from a partial restore or a full disk fails instead of decrypting as a prefix. A `Ready` document whose bytes are missing, mismatch the digest or whose scope is tombstoned (`DekDestroyed` on decrypt) fails closed with a defined problem type and an alarm. A read that finds the object gone or the scope destroyed re-reads the row first: an `Erased` row answers the gone problem type with no alarm, and only a row still not `Erased` alarms, so an erasure that commits between the read's snapshot and its open is not reported as lost evidence.
- **Mechanisms the capabilities lean on.** Ingest is a dedicated `UploadEvidence` use case; sealing is a stream-oriented sibling of `EnvelopeEncryptor` over `EncryptionScopeId` (one scope per document); erasure is `destroyScope()`. The sibling must keep the existing guarantee that sealing under a destroyed scope throws `DekDestroyed` instead of minting a fresh key; a test seals, destroys, seals again and expects refusal. **Until doc-1 closes it, that guarantee has a hole:** `DbalKeystore::destroy()` is an `UPDATE … WHERE destroyed_at IS NULL`, so on a scope never minted it affects zero rows and leaves no tombstone, and `SodiumEnvelopeEncryptor::mint()` then mints a live key for a late writer. Erasure and the staging reaper therefore leave a tombstone even when no key was minted (`destroy()` upserts a destroyed row; `kek_version` is `NOT NULL`, so the row carries a KEK version and a null `wrapped_dek`; the current version is a private constant of `SodiumEnvelopeEncryptor` that `destroy()` cannot see, so doc-1 passes it through the port or states a sentinel), and `Erased` is decided by the aggregate's compare-and-set, never by the boolean `destroyScope()` returns, which is `false` both for a repeat and for a scope that never existed. A test destroys an unminted scope, seals, and expects `DekDestroyed`. A tombstone carries a `reason` on the keystore row (`aborted-upload` or `erasure`) and is never classified by the absence of a ledger entry; the erasure ledger does not carry aborted-upload scopes, and the tombstone reconciler is driven by `Document` rows, so any non-`Erased` row whose scope is tombstoned is an alarm whatever the ledger says. **The existing `DbalSubjectErasureReconciler` must be amended in doc-1:** its query selects every destroyed `dek_keystore` row with no `SUBJECT_ERASED` audit row and filters on neither reason nor scope type, so left alone the scheduled reconciler logs every aborted-upload and every `Document` tombstone at `error` level as a GDPR divergence, for ever, burying a real one. It excludes `aborted-upload` tombstones and `Document` scopes, which the Document-driven reconciler owns; a test plants one of each and expects `unreconciledScopes()` to be empty. **Erasure converges across its stores:** the `Erased` transition, its custody row and the key destruction commit together or are re-drivable from a recorded intent, with a scenario for a crash at each point. **The scanner never makes plaintext reach persistent storage:** the scan decrypts after sealing and streams to the scanner, size limits are measured against `MAX_ORIGINAL_BYTES`, and exceeding one ends in `ScanFailed`, never `Ready`; the scanner decision below states what that means for its temporary files.
- **Decided (the user, 2026-11-04): a zero-length original is refused, and `MAX_ORIGINAL_BYTES` is 100 MiB.** What follows from it, not separate decisions: the refusal comes before the upload-intent record exists, so it leaves no keystore row and no tombstone (it is refused at the declare step of the transport decision, on a declared size of zero), and the stream seal never receives an empty stream from this use case. Exactly 104,857,600 bytes is accepted at the declare step and a larger declared size is refused there; a body that runs past the size it declared aborts the upload at the first extra byte, counted before each chunk is sealed or staged; a writer that detects the overflow itself deletes its staged file and expires its own intent under the shared lock, the reaper tombstones as usual, and the upload falls into the aborted-upload class. The constant is also the size the scanner limit is measured against: once the scanner is chosen a gate compares the two and the scanner's real limit is verified then, not assumed; a scanner whose limit is below the constant fails the gate, and resolving that mismatch is a new decision. There is no multi-gigabyte promise: CAP-3 asks for peak memory independent of size.
- **Body bounds of the upload route (a consequence, sized in doc-3).** `api/frankenphp/Caddyfile` caps every request PHP serves at `1MB` with `request_body @frontController`, matched on `index.php` after the rewrite, so the upload route needs a bound of its own, scoped to the original path (`{http.request.orig_uri.path}`) so that the 1 MB block does not also cap it (`api/config/services.yaml` says the same). Caddy's `MB` is 10^6 bytes, so the bound is written in bytes; the body is the original, so it equals the cap and needs no framing allowance. Caddy applies the bound as the body is read and promises no 413: the mandatory `Content-Length` and the end-of-stream byte count of the transport decision are what keep a body it cuts off from reading as a complete shorter original, and the application answers with a Problem Details. `post_max_size`, `upload_max_filesize` (unset in `api/frankenphp/conf.d`, so PHP's defaults apply), the upload temp directory and the request time limit are in the same set; whether they apply to a raw body is measured by the transport spike, not assumed.
- **Decided (the user, 2026-11-04): the sealed object of an `Erased` document is deleted, never kept on purpose.** Deletion is a post-commit step, idempotent and re-driven by a sweep, with an alarm on an object older than a bound measured from the `Erased` custody row; a failed delete never rolls the erasure back. What follows from it: this sweep is the single owner of the object of an `Erased` row; it removes the staged path first and then the final path, tolerates ENOENT on each and counts a row done only when neither exists, because a writer's post-commit rename can race an erasure and recreate the final object (the writer treats ENOENT on its own rename as normal); a durable marker on the snapshot, a nullable timestamp set after a verified delete with no custody row, keeps the sweep from probing every `Erased` row for ever; after a restore the sweeps stay disabled until the erasure ledger is replayed, and the delete step then removes the restored objects of already-erased documents. Offsite backups still hold the ciphertext of an erased document, a residual closed only by the key staying destroyed on every restore (the key-resurrection question below). The missing-object alarm exempting `Erased` rows holds unchanged.
- **Decided (the user, 2026-11-04): evidence upload is two steps over a raw body.** Step 1 declares the size (and optionally a sha256), carries the actor-scoped idempotency key and creates the upload-intent record; a zero or over-cap declared size is refused there, before any keystore row, so it leaves no tombstone. Step 2 is a raw-body `PUT` sealed as it is read: `Content-Length` is mandatory (411 otherwise) and must equal the declared size, and the bytes counted at end of stream must equal it, otherwise the upload falls into the aborted-upload class. No unknown-length stream exists, no multipart `UploadedFile` spool of plaintext exists for evidence, and a body Caddy cuts off cannot read as a complete shorter original. A per-actor rate limit applies to step 1 through the existing limiter pattern; an in-flight quota is deferred (YAGNI). An aborted-upload tombstone is never pruned (pruning reopens the late-writer hole) and a growth metric watches the keystore. Whether the framework hands the controller a raw stream without spooling it, and what PHP's body limits do to it, is **not verified** and is measured by a spike before doc-3.
- **Decided (the user, 2026-11-04): scan retry accounting.** "Scanner unavailable" means only that no content was sent: a health probe runs before the claim, and a down scanner leaves the document in `Received` with no claim, no epoch, no attempt and no custody row, so an outage never turns in-flight documents into `ScanFailed`. One counter on the snapshot, `scan_attempts`, counts each claim that began streaming and ended with no verdict (connection reset, scanner timeout, scanner crash, worker death, lease expiry, deadline); reaching the cap ends in `ScanFailed`. A deterministic failure goes `Scanning` to `ScanFailed` with no retry, and `Ready` is never rescanned. The scan lease is renewed by heartbeat, not scaled to size, and an absolute per-claim deadline bounds a poison document: the heartbeat compare-and-set requires `now < claimed_at + deadline`, so no heartbeat extends it. The single counter is also the ceiling on claims since the last operator decision, which resets it and increments `lease_epoch`. A sweep on `next_scan_at` in PostgreSQL drives retries and is authoritative; the Messenger message only wakes it. The heartbeat is a compare-and-set on `state = Scanning` and `lease_epoch`; a heartbeat that finds the epoch superseded or the row `Erased` aborts the scan without counting an attempt, and so does `DekDestroyed` seen mid-scan. A cap of 3, a deadline of 10 minutes and the backoff are provisional starting values fixed by measurement in doc-4.
- **Decided (the user, 2026-11-04): the scanner is ClamAV, a local `clamd` container, scanning after sealing.** The scan decrypts the original and streams it to `clamd`; `Received` stays after sealing and no pre-seal state exists. The constraint is restated from "a disk file or a spool" to **plaintext never reaches persistent storage**: `clamd` extracts archives into its temporary directory, so that directory is a `tmpfs` and swap is off, and the residual of plaintext in memory and `tmpfs` is recorded in `PRODUCTION_SECURITY_CHECKLIST.md` §7 by doc-4. **Not verified, from memory, and owned by the doc-4 spike:** `StreamMaxLength`, `MaxFileSize`, `MaxScanSize`, `MaxFiles` and `MaxRecursion`; whether `AlertExceedsMax` is off by default (exceeding a limit would then answer clean, which the `MAX_ORIGINAL_BYTES` gate must rule out); and the memory `clamd` needs against the VPS.
- **Decided (the user, 2026-11-04): the actor of `document_custody` is anonymised by one sanctioned `UPDATE`.** Sealing the actor under the subject's scope is circular (the scope id derives from the person id, so keeping it in the row is the linkable stable pseudonym that is forbidden). The statement rewrites the actor to one constant marker, never a per-subject value, is a line in the `SANCTIONED` set of the custody gate with its decision in the Documents ADR (precedent: `DbalAuditActorAnonymiser`, `docs/adr/audit-activity-log.md` D4), takes custody rows in ascending id inside the lock order above, and needs an index on the actor. The idempotency hash lives on the mutable snapshot row and is nulled by the same erasure; replay protection for that actor ends, accepted. Cost: custody is no longer absolutely append-only.
- **Decided (the user, 2026-11-04): a person's erasure never erases a document.** The uploader is not the subject, and erasing an employee must not destroy the company's evidence. It anonymises the person's references (above) and nothing else; content erasure is per document, an authorised decision through the erasure use case, with no per-subject fence and no subject scope. Erasing a whole series is a repeatable operator action that erases each non-`Erased` version in its own transaction, newest first, and reports what remains: a version filed meanwhile under a `Ready` node whose child just became `Erased` is a new live version, so the operation guarantees no termination and is not a legal guarantee.
- **Decided (the user, 2026-11-04): the erasure ledger is fixed by properties now; its adapter is chosen with the backup and restore policy.** It lives outside the PostgreSQL failure and restore domain, is append-only and independently backed up, holds scope ids only (random, not derivable from the document id), and each entry is a write-ahead intent completed forward, never rolled back. The entry is written synchronously off the VPS before `destroyScope()`, because the nightly backup's 24-hour RPO in `docs/vps-deployment.md` would otherwise bring back the key of a document erased that morning; an erasure therefore depends on the offsite channel being available (accepted: erasures are rare and the legal term is a month). A per-scope completion sweep with an alarm finishes interrupted erasures, a replay goes through the erasure use case so the snapshot, custody, the `ImageDeletionRequested` signal and the key converge, a ledger scope unknown to the restored database still gets a tombstone with reason `erasure`, and fail-closed applies globally only during a restore. The falsifier is a real restore drill that proves the key does not return. The repo has restic or borg offsite with an append-only backend and no object storage; signing the ledger is YAGNI.
- **Agreed in bulk, not asked one by one (the assistant's recommendations, 2026-11-04).** *Host:* `Documents` is a top-level context and its first consumer is BankAccount evidence, since no progress-certificate context exists. *Operator surface:* an API route behind a voter, never a console command (it would name a person on argv), with the reason as a closed enum and no free text, delivered with doc-4 because `ScanFailed` has no exit without it; the routes need entries in `api/.route-manifest.json` and `api/.credential-proof-policy`; a re-scan resets `scan_attempts` and increments `lease_epoch`. *Retention:* `retention_reason` (closed enum), `legal_basis` (GDPR Article 6 enum) and a nullable `review_at` supplied by the consumer; period, anchor, jurisdiction, policy version and enforcement wait for counsel's matrix. *Misfiling guard:* UI-only, decided at the first UI offering both entry points, with no boolean parameter on a use case.
- The contract is chosen by the use case (`UploadEvidence` vs `UploadImage`), never by a heuristic over MIME or size. The upload category joins `docs/rules/cqrs-naming.md` with the first slice.
- There is no promotion between contracts. A fungible image never becomes evidence; the supported path is re-supplying the original through `UploadEvidence`.
- `Documents` has its **own** storage port, written as a stream and sealed. No generic port spans it and `ImageStorage`.
- The upload seam is a raw request body, so the Symfony 8.1 argument resolver (`mergeParamsAndFiles`) and `Shared/Http/Infrastructure/TransportOnlyUploadedFileDenormalizer` are not on the evidence path, and the denormalizer keeps guarding the images seam. Neither a transport type (`UploadedFile`, `SplFileInfo`) nor a caller-supplied path, filename or URL reaches `Application` or the `Documents` tree; bytes arrive as a stream the Infrastructure adapter produced. A scan over the context's own tree plus a regression test that goes red when the scan stops matching enforces it, because deptrac cannot express it.
- **Ingest is atomic from the caller's view.** Bytes land under a staging key and are promoted only when the stream is complete and matches any declared length or digest; an upload intent whose lease expired with no row is reaped: its staged object is deleted and its scope tombstoned. A replayed `UploadEvidence` carries an actor-scoped idempotency key and returns the existing document, which is not deduplication; the same key with different bytes is a conflict, and a replay after the original became `Erased` returns the tombstone, never a revived document. Bytes are compared by digest first, at the declare step when a digest was declared and at end of stream otherwise, so a mismatch is a conflict even against an `Erased` original. The declare step takes the `UNIQUE` on the actor-scoped key (the intent row holds the key hash until the document row that carries it commits), so two concurrent first uploads under one key do not both stream: the loser is answered there with the existing intent or document and has no scope and no bytes.
- **Two digests with different meanings:** the original's (integrity, custody, signature) and, for images, the canonical image's (visual identity, caching). Neither is an identity or a uniqueness key; no unique index on a digest.
- **Every state change is a compare-and-set on the aggregate and `Erased` rejects all of them.** A late scan verdict or a late derivative never revives or outlives an erased document. The compare-and-set of a scan transition also carries a monotonic `lease_epoch`, incremented on every claim and on an operator reset, never reset and independent of the retry counter (the attempt number is not the token), so a verdict from a superseded lease is dropped and cannot override the verdict of the scanner that now holds it. The expiry that returns `Scanning` to `Received` is itself a compare-and-set whose predicate is `state = Scanning AND lease_epoch = e AND lease_expires_at < NOW()`, evaluated inside the `UPDATE` by the database clock, so it cannot expire a lease a late verdict just completed or one a heartbeat has just renewed.
- Erasure keeps nothing that names a person. A person's erasure anonymises their references (uploader, custody actor, upload-intent actor, idempotency hash) and never erases a document. Free-text fields that can carry a name (original filename, title) are either sealed under the uploader's scope, so they die with that person's scope while the document's bytes and digest stay, or forbidden as free text; what survives in custody is digest, timestamps, the anonymised actor marker and legal basis. The decision for each is taken in the story that first persists the field (doc-2 for the schema, doc-3 for the values), never after: a plaintext filename persisted between those stories would outlive the erasure. Each such field names the scope that seals it, because the document's bytes die with the document's scope while a person's fields die with theirs.
- A consumer's `*DocumentId` field that references a natural person's evidence is personal data in that consumer: mark it `#[PersonalData]` so `PiiDiffSealer` seals it under the subject's scope. The document's own id is not marked. Classify the `Document` resource type in `api/.audit-resource-types`.
- A column holding a person id needs a named erasure owner (`api/.person-reference-policy`, `#[PersonSubjectReference]` and a `PersonReferenceSource`), registered in the story that introduces the column, never later. The uploader on the snapshot, the actor column of `document_custody`, the actor-scoped idempotency key, the actor of the upload-intent record (which exists before any `Document` row and dies with the intent or is purged on the actor's erasure) and the operator named by an operator-decision custody row are such columns.
- **Derivative removal rides the existing signal.** `Shared.Image` is classified `person` in `api/.persistent-transport-policy` and `ImageDeletionRequested` routes to `async` under `docs/adr/image-deletion-signal-transport.md`, which closes no erasure risk and records its own residuals (the id stays in `event_store`; a dead letter swept at 30 days drops the instruction). The `Document` aggregate stays unrouted; the payload carries image ids only. Removal is never synchronous inside the owner's transaction, retries re-query by origin rather than from a list built once, and an alarm fires on derivatives of an erased document that outlive a bound.
- An `ImageId` is held by exactly one aggregate instance. `Documents` is the holder of its derivative ids.
- Other contexts reference a document by id only; reads compose through an explicit DQL JOIN projection.
- Evidence semantics (retention, legal basis, what counts as proof) live in `Documents`, never in `Shared/`.
- The first merged code in which another context materialises a rendition through the images module is the ADR D4 trigger to decide renaming `Images`; that story records the decision.
- Every story touching erasure or derivative lifecycle records an adversarial pass; the three-layer review is owed per story.

## Non-goals

- No code, schema or route is produced by this spec; it fixes guidelines and a story order.
- No deduplication, shared blob identity, reference counting or GC (ADR invariant 3).
- No transformation of an original, and no virus scanning, OCR or versioning inside the images module.
- No migration of images into evidence; no best-effort original kept in the images module.
- No S3, object-lock or other non-local backend, and no hash-chaining of custody, in the first slices.
- Retention is recorded but not enforced: no schedule erases on expiry. Erasure is on request until a story says otherwise (it would need a schedule consumer on a single replica).

## Success signal

A first `Documents` story can start from this folder without reopening the fungible-vs-evidence boundary, the storage-port split, the erasure mechanism or the story order, and with the open questions below answered or consciously deferred. In particular, a demonstration where an uploaded drawing is stored byte-identical, is unreadable until scanned, and after erasure leaves digest and custody while every derivative is gone.

## Assumptions

- `Documents` lives outside `Shared/`, because evidence semantics are business meaning (ADR D4); it is a top-level context (see the decisions agreed in bulk).
- The `#268` item "`UploadedDocument` port wrapping the request file" is superseded by ADR invariant 6.
- Fail closed while the scanner is unavailable.
- `Shared/Images` has no origin on `Image` and no entry point for another context to materialise a derivative; the derivatives story creates both.
